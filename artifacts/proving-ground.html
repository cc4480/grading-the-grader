Proving Ground
Build plan · secscan.me

Stop asking people to believe you.

Every scanner vendor publishes a number. None of them publishes a target you can check it against. secscan.me is that target — a deliberately vulnerable application, hosted publicly, with its complete fault list published. Anyone can point any scanner at it and score the result themselves, including ours.

01 — The problem this solves

Scanner accuracy claims are unfalsifiable, so nobody believes any of them

Including ours, and reasonably so.

A scanner vendor says it finds 95% of vulnerabilities. Against what? Measured how? Nobody can check, because the measurement needs a target whose true fault list is known, and no vendor hands one over. So every accuracy claim in this industry is a number with no denominator, and experienced buyers have learned to discount all of them equally.

That is the actual reason a new scanner is not taken seriously. Not the quality of the work — the uncheckability of the claim. A better number does not fix it. Only a checkable one does.

The claim that survives scrutiny is not “we scored 92%”. It is “here is the target, here is the fault list, score us yourself.”
02 — What already exists

The measurement has been run. It just cannot be independently reproduced yet.

A benchmark was built and executed on 2026-09-22 against a local instance. Three scanners, one target, 74 planted faults, adjudicated blind.

Detection against 74 planted faults · blind adjudication, 3 judges
SecScan 68/74
OWASP ZAP 18/74
Nuclei 9/74

219 of 222 adjudication decisions were unanimous. The three disputes were all “unclear” versus a call, never yes versus no.

Why this is not yet publishable as-is

The judges are not independent. They are three instances of one model, sharing weights and priors, so they fail the same way when they fail. High agreement between them is close to what you would predict by construction, and is much weaker evidence than it looks.

The target is our own checklist. The fixture was built to contain the faults SecScan checks for. A tool scoring lower against it has been shown to overlap less with our checklist — not to be worse. ZAP reported 11 findings our fault list does not contain at all.

Nobody was equipped equally. Five faults need two authenticated sessions or an OpenAPI spec; none of the three tools was given either. That is why SecScan scores 68 here and 73 in its own credentialed verification run.

Publishing the scoreboard alone would be a vendor benchmark, and would be dismissed as one. Publishing the target is what converts it into evidence.

03 — The mechanism

Why a public target makes the claim bulletproof

Not because it makes our number bigger. Because it removes us from the measurement.

  • The mapping stops being ours. Today, deciding whether ZAP's “Cross-Domain JavaScript Source File Inclusion” counts as detecting path traversal is a judgement we make about a competitor. Against a public target with a published manifest, every reader makes that judgement themselves.
  • Our mistakes become checkable rather than hidden. An earlier pass of our own mapping scored ZAP at 59%, then 47%, then 31%, purely on regex choices. That history gets published too. A benchmark that shows its own corrections is far harder to dismiss than one that appears to have got it right first time.
  • Competitors can dispute it on the record. A scanner vendor who thinks their score is unfair can re-run it and publish a rebuttal. That exchange is the strongest possible advertisement for the claim, whoever wins.
  • It compounds. Public vulnerable targets get cited, linked and used in teaching for years. testphp.vulnweb.com and scanme.nmap.org have each done this for over a decade. That is durable authority no marketing spend buys.
04 — What gets published

The four artefacts, in priority order

ArtefactWhy it has to be public
The fault manifest
/manifest.json, versioned		 The denominator. Every planted fault, with an id, a plain-language description, its location and its category. Without this the site is just a scannable box.
The target itself
https://secscan.me		 Reachable by any scanner, no signup, no key, no rate-limit that breaks a normal scan.

all three tools, unedited		 So a reader can compare their own run against ours and see whether we reported honestly.
The adjudication, including its errors The mapping, the judges' verdicts, and the record of our scores changing as the mapping was corrected. This is the credibility lever, not an embarrassment to bury.

An explicit authorisation notice ships alongside them, on the scanme.nmap.org model: this host exists to be scanned, and you may scan it.

One wrinkle worth designing around now

Two of the planted faults are “security.txt is missing” and “robots.txt discloses sensitive paths”. The two conventional places to put a “this is a deliberate test target” notice would each break a fault the manifest promises. The notice goes in the page banner, a dedicated /about page, and a DNS TXT record instead.

05 — Safety

What has to be true before this is exposed

Most of the fixture is already safe to publish. One endpoint is not.

Already safe — the faults are simulated

SQL injection: no database exists; the endpoint returns a believable SQLITE_ERROR string. Path traversal: checks for .. and returns a hardcoded fake /etc/passwd — no file is ever read. Secrets: provably fake literals (sk_live_51JvFake, sk_live_51Source). Exposed files: static decoy content.

Blocking — the SSRF endpoint is real

/api/webhook-preview?url= performs a live fetch() of any URL a stranger supplies, with no allowlist and no scheme restriction. Public, that is an open proxy into cloud metadata, internal networks and port scanning attributed to our IP. It must be constrained to a single allowlisted collector domain, with egress blocked at the container level to link-local and RFC1918 ranges, before deployment.

  • Total isolation. Its own project, its own container, zero cloud credentials, no shared network. Never the infrastructure secscan.us runs on.
  • Edge rate limiting so it cannot be used as a DDoS reflector, tuned to permit a normal scan and refuse a flood.
  • Assume constant attack. Public vulnerable apps are found within hours. That is acceptable given the above, but it is an ongoing commitment, not a deploy-and-forget.
  • Fixed, reproducible content. If the app drifts, published results stop matching what a reader sees. Manifest and app version together, and the version appears in every scan report.
06 — Build order

Five phases

01

Harden the SSRF endpoint

Allowlist a single collector host, block link-local and private ranges at the egress layer, keep the fault detectable by the probe that is supposed to find it. Re-run verify-vuln-fixture.ts to confirm the planted fault still fires.

02

Generate the manifest from the fixture

Derive manifest.json from the same source of truth the verify script uses, so the published fault list cannot drift from the app. Version it alongside the app.

generated, never hand-written
03

Deploy behind Cloudflare

Isolated project, no credentials, rate limiting at the edge, authorisation banner, /about page, DNS TXT notice.

infraisolation
04

Re-run the benchmark against the public host

All three scanners against secscan.me rather than localhost, so the published figures describe the target a reader can actually reach. Expect the numbers to move; publish the movement.

must precede publication
05

Publish, and invite the rebuttal

Results, raw data, mapping, adjudication and correction history. Explicitly invite other vendors to re-run and dispute. Submit the target to the places security people look for practice ranges.

07 — Claims

What we may say, and what we may not

The discipline that makes the rest of it credible. One overclaim discredits every honest number on the page.

ClaimAllowedWhy
“Against a public target with 74 published faults, SecScan detected 68. Run it yourself.” YES Reproducible by anyone, denominator published
“We publish our false-positive history and our benchmark corrections.” YES True, and checkable
“Ask any scanner you are considering for its detection rate against known ground truth.” YES A challenge, not a comparative claim
“SecScan is 3.8× better than ZAP.” NO The target is our own checklist; lower overlap is not lower quality
“Superior to any scanner out there.” NO Most scanners have never been run; unfalsifiable and legally exposed
“Independently verified.” NO The adjudicators were three instances of one model, not independent parties

The three refusals are what make the three permissions land. A reader who finds one honest limitation stated up front extends credit to everything around it; a reader who catches one overclaim discounts the lot.
